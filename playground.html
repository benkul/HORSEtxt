the trace — what the horse attended to

Ears are attention (GRAMMAR.md §4; Wathan & McComb 2014, Current Biology 24(15)). Flattened, the trace retreats — the animal is not attending, so neither should you. One ear each way is divided attention, drawn as a split fork: the left eye reads novelty, the right reads category, because the hemispheres do different work (§12g, bibliography “Laterality”). Head height is part of the attention display; the trace carries it literally — high head, frames float; low head, frames sink.

The same stream the trace reads, drawn as the animal. Ears are attention (EAD101 forward / EAD103 flattening, EquiFACS — Wathan et al. 2015): a flattened ear is agonistic and the horse is not attending, so the whole body dims and retreats (GRAMMAR.md §4; runtime attending = ears !== "agonistic"). Ears aim independently on ten muscles, 180° (Wathan & McComb 2014), so one ear each way is divided attention and the two eyes look different ways, because the hemispheres do different work (§12g). Head height is part of the attention display (Wathan & McComb 2014): a high head lifts or rears, a low head sinks. Nostril flare, lid droop, tail carriage and tension all trace to the ethogram (Lewis et al. 2025) and the reinforcement spine.
